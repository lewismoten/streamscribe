# extract-thumbnails

Script: [`extract-thumbnails.js`](../../src/media/thumbnails/extract-thumbnails.js)

Command:

```bash
npm run extract-thumbnails -- [--session <folder>] [options]
```

Saves small thumbnails across a captured live session in passes that refine the spread: one every 5 minutes, then the midpoints (every 2.5 minutes), then their midpoints (75 seconds), and so on down to `--min-interval`. Stopping early still leaves an even spread, and re-running continues where the last run stopped (existing thumbnails are kept).

It is fast because each 10-second HLS segment is its own small file with a keyframe about every second: a thumbnail opens only that segment, jumps to the nearest keyframe before the target, decodes one frame at thumbnail size, and several run in parallel. A 3-hour meeting (1,148 thumbnails down to every 9.4 seconds) takes about 15 seconds; the 5-minute spread is ready in under a second.

Output goes to `{session}/thumbnails/`:

- `HH-MM-SS.jpg`, named by video position (matching the transcript timestamps)
- `scenes/scene-HH-MM-SS.jpg` and `scenes.json`: camera and slide changes. After the thumbnails, the session's keyframes (about one a second) are compared at low resolution in parallel, and a frame that differs sharply from the one before (ffmpeg scene score above 0.3) counts as a change; a change that lands back on the same view as the previous one (such as a brief flash or camera wobble) is dropped. Keyframes come about once a second, so each change is then moved to its exact frame: every frame in the second and a half before it is compared, and the biggest change wins. The change's picture is taken half a second into the new shot, once a dissolve has settled. This takes about 25 seconds for a 2½-hour meeting; when more segments arrive, only the new ones are scanned.
- `cards.json` and `cards/card-HH-MM-SS.jpg`: title cards. The same pass finds still stretches, where the picture doesn't change at all from one keyframe to the next and the sound stays below −50 dB for at least 20 seconds. A live camera always changes a little, even on a quiet room; a card shown while the microphones are off doesn't. A still stretch of 30 seconds or more counts as a card. Its picture is saved and its text read with macOS's built-in text recognition, where available (compiled once into `data/state/bin/ocr`; it needs Xcode's command line tools). When a card ends, two chapters are added: one where it starts, named by its text (for example **Executive Session (closed)**, for a board meeting in closed session), and **Back in open session** (or **Resumed**) where the meeting returns. A chapter already marked within 30 seconds is kept instead, and each card is handled once, so an edited or removed chapter stays that way. The library also never uses a card as a recording's picture.
- `thumbnails.json`: each thumbnail's video position, approximate time of day, segment sequence, and pass (`level` 0 is every 5 minutes)
- `index.html`: the thumbnails page. Left of the slider, **↔ All** / **📑 Chapter** sets what it covers: the whole meeting, or only the chapter being played (from its start to the next chapter's; the scrubber turns purple, and its tooltip names the chapter). In Chapter mode, markers above the scrubber show who is speaking (a photo at each change, +N for several), ✋ where each motion was made, and 🗳 where each vote's voting begins (click to watch the roll call; ringed green if it passed, dotted red if it failed); hover for details and click to jump. The transcript shows only that chapter, both follow playback into the next chapter, dragging stops just short of the next one, and the choice is remembered. Under the slider is one row of icon buttons, each with a tooltip: ▶︎/⏸ play, the position (click it, type a time, and press Enter to go there: a video time such as 1:04:44, 64:44, or 3884 seconds, or a time of day such as 2:19 PM; Escape puts it back), ◀ ▶ frame step, ✂⟦ 🎬 ⟧✂ clip start, download clip, and clip end (🎬 opens **Download clip**, which can be widened by dragging its corner: refine the start and end by dragging the edges on the audio waveform or on the strip of frames above it (clicking elsewhere moves the playhead there, kept within the clip: a click outside it, or scrubbing past an edge, stops at that edge; ▶ Play clip picks up from it; 🔁 Loop starts over at the end), zoom out and in with − + (or by scrolling over the waveform), ▶ play the clip, choose frame-exact if needed, and ⬇ Download; with **Include the overlays** checked, the browser instead plays the clip and records it with everything drawn on the video (clock, name, chapter, speakers, votes, magnified speakers) and the sound including any playback boost, taking as long as the clip (⏹ stops early) and saving MP4 where the browser can, otherwise WebM; otherwise the local server cuts it with `extract-clip` into the session's `clips/` folder and the browser downloads it), 📷 save frame, 🎞 frames, ◀ 🗣️ ▶ speakers (the arrows jump to the previous and next speaker change), ◀ 📑 ▶ chapters (📑 shows or hides the Chapters list; the arrows jump to the start of this or the previous chapter, and to the next one), ◀ 🗳 ▶ votes (🗳 shows or hides the Votes list; the arrows jump to the previous and next vote), and 🎛 display settings, followed by the boost and status text. The 🎛 dialog holds what to show on the video (🕐 clock and video time, meeting name, chapter, speakers, votes), each with a text size slider (50–250%), plus 🔊 Boost quiet speakers and **Reset sizes and positions**. It opens in the top right corner without dimming the page, so changes can be watched as they're made (🎛 again closes it). This and the other dialogs move by dragging their heading (⠿). Drag any overlay on the video to move it (a press that doesn't move is still a click). Sizes and positions are remembered in the browser and scale with the video, and saved 📷 frames show the overlays exactly where and as large as they appear; the options described below as **Overlay**, **Show …**, and **Boost quiet speakers** are there. Play the captured video (or drag the slider, or click a thumbnail) to move through the meeting; ✂⟦ (green) and ⟧✂ (red) mark a clip's start and end, shown above the scrubber as a green and a red ▼ (click to go there) with the scrubber highlighted between them, and 🎬 downloads it. The grid shows thumbnails at a chosen density (every 5 minutes down to all of them). Space toggles play/pause, and so does clicking the video (hovering it shows a large ▶︎ or ⏸), except on the overlays that are clicked to edit or record. **Overlay** shows the time of day each moment aired (time only, since the date belongs in the meeting name; the weekday and date are added once a meeting runs past midnight) (approximate, in `locale.timeZone`), the video time, or both on the video area; the page remembers the choice. The 📷 button pauses the video and downloads the current frame as a full-resolution PNG named by video position (for example `frame-01-04-44.png`), with the clock drawn in when it is shown; the browser saves the frame it already has decoded, so no server work is involved. Once the player has loaded, ◀ ▶ (or the `,` and `.` keys) step one frame at a time, and 🎞 **Frames** pauses and opens a strip of 25 frames centered on the current one, spaced 1, 5, or 15 frames apart; click a frame to jump to it, then save it with 📷. When the session has a transcript, it appears beside the video (below it on narrow screens), as tall as the video with its scrubber and toolbar, and pinned while the page scrolls; the find box sits under the current camera image: the line being spoken is highlighted and kept in view, a large image above the transcript shows the camera or slide on screen at the current moment, switching the instant a change happens (click it to jump to that change), and a small image of each change sits on its own row between the lines where it happened, clicking any line jumps the video to when it was spoken, and the find box searches it: ◀ ▶ beside it (or Shift+Enter / Enter) step through the results. In Chapter mode the current chapter's results come first, then the rest of the meeting, and a result in another chapter moves the video there so that chapter is shown. Served pages load the latest transcript, so new corrections show without rebuilding. The address bar keeps the position and clip range (for example `?t=01:04:44.100&from=01:02:30&to=01:10:00`), so refreshing, bookmarking, or sharing the link reopens the page at the same spot.
- Speakers (on the served page): 🗣️ **Speakers** opens a panel of known people. People are shown in sections. **🗳 Voting members** of this meeting come first, highlighted and in seating order, then groups (Elected officials, County staff, Residents, Vendors, and Other organizations to start), then Not grouped. Drag a photo onto a section to move that person there; dropping on Voting members makes them vote in this meeting, and dragging them out takes that away. **➕ Group** adds a group (VDOT, for example), clicking a group's name renames it, and an empty group has ✕ to remove it. The person editor also has a **Group** field. Groups are saved with the people list (`people/people.json`), so every meeting shares them. The panel starts in **🙂 Icons** view: photos only, each group a compact block with its name above its circles, side by side (hover a photo for the name, and for ✎ to edit). **🪪 Names** switches to full-width sections with names and titles. The choice is remembered. Pause or play to a moment and click the people who are talking (one or more; click again to unmark, **Nobody** for silence); that set holds from that moment until the next change, and clicks within a second of each other make one change. The ◀ ▶ beside 🗣️ in the toolbar jump between marks, and **Remove the change here** deletes one. **➕ Add person** grabs the current video frame at full resolution and opens an editor: drag the circle onto their face (or click where it should go), resize it with the slider or mouse wheel, and enter their name and role (Supervisor, County Administrator, and so on; suggestions offered). The person is saved and marked as speaking. Each person's **🕑** (beside ✎) opens **When they spoke**: how many times and how long in all, a bar across the meeting with their stretches (click to jump), and a list of each stretch with its time, length, and first words (click to jump, 🔊 to boost it, ✂ to make it the clip). ◀ ▶ step to their previous and next stretch, and the stretch being played is highlighted. For someone who isn't on screen, **🚫 No photo** skips the photo (or removes an existing one); they show as initials. When a name isn't known, check **Name not known** and give a role: the role shows in place of the name everywhere (cards, overlays, votes, transcript), or "Unknown" without one. People named "Unknown" (or left blank) are treated the same way. An entry that stands for several people, such as **All present** (everyone reciting the Pledge in unison), can have an **Icon** (an emoji such as 👥) in place of a photo, and shows its full name in short labels. ✎ edits a person later, including retaking the photo from whatever frame is showing. The transcript reads like a conversation. Voting members' words are on the right, with their photo on the right; everyone else's are on the left. Words flow together, across Whisper's lines, for as long as the same people are speaking. A new speaker starts a new bubble at the word where they began, and a pause of 2 seconds or more starts a new paragraph. Bubbles carry no times; instead the video time and time of day sit centered between them as each minute begins (click one to go there). Chapter headings, votes, camera changes, and gaps also start a new bubble. Hover a paragraph for 🔊 to boost it and transcribe it again; words from a boosted re-transcription are tinted. **⬇** beside the find box downloads the transcript as text, for the whole meeting or the chapter being played; each chapter in the Chapters list has its own ⬇ too. The text file starts with the meeting name (and **Chapter:** with the chapter's name), puts **== Chapter ==** at each chapter break in a whole-meeting download, and marks the time of day as each minute begins, as **[02:19:46 PM]**. Each line names who is speaking, with their role ("Lewis Moten, North River Resident: Hello Supervisors"); a new line starts at each speaker change and pause. Motions, seconds, and votes appear where they happened, each vote with its result and how each member voted. Word corrections are applied. The word being said is highlighted as the video plays. Word times are estimated by sharing each line's time across its words, since whisper times whole lines. Click any word to jump there and pick who starts speaking at it: voting members first, then people who have spoken in this meeting, then everyone else. Cmd/Ctrl-click picks several people speaking together. **Nobody** marks no one in particular, and **Remove the change here** undoes a change at that word, which shows a red mark at its left edge. The same picker corrects the word. Type the right word and press Enter (**✎ Correct**); several words replace it, which is how to add words after it. **🗑** deletes it, and **↺** restores what was transcribed. Corrected words have a dotted underline, with the original on hover. Deleted words stay visible, struck through and faded, so they can be restored. Corrections are saved in `{session}/word-edits.json` (and the library database), each tied to its line, its word number, and the word as transcribed, so they apply to the transcript they were made on (the usual one, or the best one). Library search uses the corrected words of the usual transcript. Wherever nothing was transcribed for 8 seconds or more, a row says **⏸ No words for …**, since that may be silence or speech too quiet to make out. Click it to go there, or **🔊 Boost & re-transcribe** to open the boost dialog for exactly that stretch. When a session has a `best` transcript (`npm run transcribe -- --best --output best`), the menu beside the find box switches to it; its words have their own times from whisper.cpp instead of estimates. Speakers' faces appear beside the first bubble of each change, their faces sit on the lower right of the image above the transcript while they are talking, and **Show speakers** overlays a card for each one (photo, name, role) on the lower left of the video, also drawn into 📷 frames. Saving goes through the streamscribe server (`npm start`), which keeps it in the library database (`data/streamscribe.db`) and also writes:
  - `{session}/speakers.json`: `turns`, each `{ at: video position, speakers: [person ids] }`
  - `{source storage}/people/people.json` and `people/<id>.png` (256×256 circle crops): the roster, shared by every meeting from the source so each person is set up once
- Boost & re-transcribe (on the served page; 🔊 in the toolbar): click a speaker's face (beside the transcript or on the image above it) to set the clip to everything they say in that stretch, from the mark where they start to the mark where they stop, and open the dialog right away; or, with any clip range set, **🔊 Boost & re-transcribe…** opens a dialog to raise the volume of that portion, listen live or to the server's exact version, and have it transcribed again. Boosts can be saved, and **Boost quiet speakers** plays them wherever they apply. See [`retranscribe-range`](../transcription/retranscribe-range.md).
- Chapters (on the served page), such as a meeting's agenda items: the **Chapters** list under the video holds chapters that start at given times. Type a title and press **Add**: it goes at the current position, or at a time you type (**⏱ Now** fills in the current one). Click an item to jump there; the item being played is highlighted and kept in view. A long agenda (or list of votes) scrolls within its own box. **📑 Chapter** (in 🎛) overlays the chapter being played on the top left of the video (below the meeting name when that's shown too), and it's drawn into 📷 frames. ✎ edits an item's title or time, and ✕ removes it. Each item also appears as a heading in the transcript, just before the line where it starts (click it to jump). Chapters are saved in `{session}/agenda.json`. A full meeting from `build-meeting` brings in the sessions' agenda items until you add or change one on the meeting's page, and `split-session` splits them.
- Votes (on the served page), in the **Votes** section under the video:
  - **Voting members…** lists everyone in the people list; check the ones who vote in this meeting. If someone leaves early, move to that moment and press **Left here**, and later votes start them as absent. **Arrived here** does the same for someone who comes late. **🗳＋ Vote starts now** (in the Votes list) starts a new vote at the current moment and turns on Show votes, so you can click members on the video as they vote; the overlay's hover buttons also have **⏱ Starts now** to move a vote's start. **Seating order** lists the voting members left to right. Use ◀ ▶ to match where they sit on camera; the vote overlay, the roll call, and 📷 frames follow that order.
  - **🗳 Record a vote here** opens a vote panel at the current position. It floats in a corner so the video keeps playing and scrubbing. Say what the motion is (it starts as just "Motion") and, optionally, who moved and seconded it and when. Picking a person fills in when they last started speaking before the vote, and **⏱ Now** uses the current position.
  - Roll call: as each member votes, move to that moment and click their photo. Each click records their vote at that moment, cycling aye → nay → abstain → absent → not voted. Clicks within two seconds change the same entry, and each member shows their recorded changes with times. **Everyone present: aye, now** records ayes for everyone still in the meeting. Members who left before the vote opened count as absent.
  - Outcome: a motion passes as soon as the needed number of ayes is in (3 on a 5-seat board). It fails once nays plus absences make that impossible (3 on a 5-seat board), or when everyone has voted without enough ayes, for example because of abstentions. Until then it's still voting. Set **Seats** and **Ayes needed to pass** under Voting members; by default, seats are the number of voting members and a majority is needed. **Outcome** can override the final result.
  - Votes are listed with their result and who moved and seconded (click to jump, ✎ to edit or remove). The transcript gets a row where the motion was made, where it was seconded, and where the vote was taken.
  - **Show votes** puts a panel on the top right of the video from when the vote opens until 5 seconds after it passes or fails, then fades it out over a second. A vote that's never decided stays up 20 seconds (set per vote) after its last recorded vote. It shows the motion, who moved and seconded it, and each member's photo with their full last name and district (from their role, such as "Supervisor, South River District"), all on one row, filling in as the roll call plays: a ✔ (aye), ✖ (nay), or – (abstain) badge, faded while they haven't voted, more faded when absent. The tally shows "Voting: 2 aye, 1 nay…" until the motion passes or fails. The panel on the video is clickable. Clicking a member's photo records their vote at that moment, cycling like the roll call. Hovering shows **🗳 Vote / ✋ Moved / ✋ Seconded**: pick Moved or Seconded, then click the member who made or seconded the motion, and it's recorded at that moment (clicking the same person again within two seconds clears it). Clicking Moved or Seconded under the motion picks that mode too. With the vote panel closed, clicks save right away. While it's open, they change the vote being edited, which stays on the video the whole time, so you can scrub back to the motion before the vote opened. Saved 📷 frames never include the hover buttons. Click the motion on the panel to edit it in place (Enter saves, Escape cancels). It's drawn into 📷 frames too.
  - Everything is saved in `{session}/votes.json`. A full meeting from `build-meeting` brings in the sessions' votes until you save one on the meeting's page, and `split-session` splits them.
- Magnify (on the served page): show a person larger, in a box you place, while the rest of the video stays visible.
  - **Camera views:** the **📷** menu on the camera image above the transcript says which view the camera is on. **➕ New view from this camera** names one (for example "Dais, wide"). Camera changes that look like it are matched automatically (by comparing small grayscale copies of the pictures in the browser: shots from the same angle differ by a few levels, other angles by 40 or more), and the menu can set any shot to a view, or to "not a view", by hand.
  - **✎ Zoom areas…** (in that menu) opens the editor on the current video frame. Pick a person (voting members first, anyone else from the menu) and drag a square around where they sit; a larger copy appears. **▢ Where they sit** / **⧉ Larger copy** switch which box is being edited (while editing where they sit, the larger copy is hidden; while editing the copy, the square shows as a dashed outline; names sit just outside the boxes): drag inside it to move it, or drag any corner or edge handle to resize it (it keeps its shape). In Where they sit, dragging on an empty spot draws a new square; the larger copy keeps its place and size.
  - With **🔍 Magnify whoever is speaking** checked in 🎛 (saving zoom areas turns it on), whenever that person speaks while the camera is on that view, their larger copy grows out of where they sit just before they start (about 0.8 seconds, fading in), so it's in place when they begin. It stays about 3 seconds after they stop, then shrinks back into their seat as it fades out (timed by the video, so scrubbing and pausing agree). All of this stays within the camera shot it belongs to, worked out for the exact frame. A copy never grows in before a cut to the shot where the person speaks, and it never lingers past a cut away. If someone is already talking when the camera cuts to their view, their copy grows in at the start of the new shot. Copies have a black border and a drop shadow.
  - **🔍** in the toolbar magnifies a square chosen by hand (drag on the video); 🔍 again removes it.
  - Saved 📷 frames include whatever is magnified, at full resolution. Views and areas are saved in `{session}/views.json`.
- Playlist (on the served page): collect clips and make one video of them.
  - **Adding clips:** mark a clip with ✂⟦ and ⟧✂, press **➕** to add it, then mark the next one, and so on. **📼** shows or hides the playlist, which lists each clip with its times and length, and the total. A clip is named after its chapter, or else its first words.
  - **Arranging:** click a clip to go there (with the cut marks set to it), ↑ ↓ to reorder, ✎ to rename, and ✕ to remove.
  - **▶ Play all** plays the clips one after another.
  - **⬇ Video:** the server cuts each clip frame-exact and joins them in order into one MP4 (`npm run render-playlist`), saved in `{session}/clips/` and downloaded.
  - **⬇ Transcript:** what is said in each clip, as text, under a heading for each clip.
  - **⬇ Captions (.srt):** closed captions timed to the playlist video, for YouTube. Each caption has at most two lines of about 42 characters and lasts up to 6 seconds. A new caption starts at each speaker change, named, and at each pause. The ⬇ menu beside the find box has the same captions for the whole meeting.
  - The playlist is saved in `{session}/playlist.json` (and the library database).
- Meeting name (on the served page): check **Show meeting name** and click the name on the top left of the video to edit it (it shows "Meeting name" until one is set). The name becomes the browser tab's title and is saved in `{session}/meeting-info.json`. It's drawn into 📷 frames too. A full meeting from `build-meeting` starts with the first named session's name.
- `../playback.m3u8`: an HLS playlist of the session's captured segments, used by the page's player

Playing video: browsers can't play the `.ts` segments directly, so the page streams them through `playback.m3u8` (natively in Safari, with hls.js elsewhere). Chrome and Firefox block that on a page opened from disk (`file://`), so run `npm start` and open the page at the address it shows (the page links to it). Scrubbing through thumbnails and building clip commands work either way.

Options:

- `--session <folder>` (default: the most recently updated capture)
- `--first-interval 300`: seconds between thumbnails in the first pass
- `--min-interval 5`: stop once the spacing would drop below this many seconds
- `--width 320`: thumbnail width in pixels
- `--concurrency N`: how many to extract at once (default: the number of CPU cores)
- `--watch [seconds]`: keep going while the session is still being captured, checking for new segments every 10 seconds (or the number given). Each check adds thumbnails and camera changes for the new segments only, and keeps `playback.m3u8` open (an HLS event playlist), so the served page can play and scrub everything captured so far: it picks up new thumbnails, camera changes, segments, and transcript lines from `thumbnails/live.json` every few seconds, and **● Live** beside the position jumps to the latest moment. When the capture starts a new session folder (Swagit renews its stream identifier about hourly), the session's page and playlist are finished and the new session is watched instead (with `--session`, watching stops there). Watching ends when no segment has arrived for `--idle-minutes`, or on Ctrl+C.
- `--idle-minutes 90`: with `--watch`, how long without new segments before stopping (long enough for a closed session or recess)

Moments that were missed or discarded during capture have no thumbnail. `split-session` moves thumbnails to the session they belong to splits the speaker marks (whoever was speaking at the split carries over), and clears the scene changes, which the next run of this script detects again for each session.
